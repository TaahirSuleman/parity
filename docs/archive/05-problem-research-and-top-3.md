# Build Callback, Not Shockwave, to Win

The strongest project for this solo Indie-track entry is **Callback**. Callback is a multimodal multi-agent system that takes yesterday's recorded IT help-desk calls, the agent's screen recordings and the identity-provider logs that followed. It works out which password or MFA resets were social engineering, then rolls them back behind a calibrated gate that a human must acknowledge. It scores **53/60** on the constitution's Article VIII table, has no hard-fail, and is the only candidate that passes all four recipe filters. Three things put it there. The loss is large and current: abuse of help-desk processes sits behind the £270–440M M&S/Co-op event, vishing is now the #2 intrusion vector, and it is the costliest vector at $5.29M per breach. No vendor checked claims the exact post-call job. And the product needs four Meta capabilities at once: Voice diarization, Spark perception grounding, SAM 3.1 tracking and Spark computer use. **Compliance Shockwave 1.0 as written scores 32/60 and fails the hard-fail criterion on Meta tooling.** SAP GTS, Descartes and e2open already re-screen open orders when a list changes, Castellum shipped agentic alert adjudication in September 2026, and invoice extraction is generic document AI. Its best pivot, multimodal UFLPA admissibility packs, reaches only 41 because the ground truth is weak, footage rights are hard to get and the participant has no trade background. The two other finalists are **Claim Clock** (UK APP scam reimbursement adjudication, 47) and **First Notice** (multi-regime incident reporting from war-room evidence, 48). Claim Clock has the best-verified whitespace and the only real outcome labels. First Notice has the strongest dated forcing functions but risks being read as a DORA sequel. Recipe v3 keeps the Gatekeepers story machine and adds two things. The first is what 2025–26 grand-prize winners had and compliance runners-up lacked: non-text evidence that decides the outcome, plus a named verifier and a named gate. The second is the measured evidence that almost no winner showed. The evidence is thinnest in one decisive place: no source publishes per-case investigation times for any finalist, so impact must be built from a timed manual baseline. Commit to Callback only after a one-day spike on its three riskiest steps, a whitespace re-check and an IP-clause check.

## Recipe v3: keep the story machine, add perception and proof

The overall winners at the large Google, AWS, Microsoft, Cerebral Valley and Meta-judged events reviewed share a profile that the Gatekeepers only half had. **Every 2025–26 agent grand prize but one put a non-text modality at the centre.**

- **ORION** won the Gemini Live Agent Challenge (11,878 participants) as a solo entry. It fused a surgeon's voice with surgical video, and its page claims Gemini Live is "the only model that supports simultaneous PCM audio input + audio output + function calling + image streaming" ([Google Cloud Blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/winners-and-highlights-of-the-gemini-live-agent-challenge); [Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/orion-operating-room-intelligent-orchestration-node)).
- **Globot** won the Gemini 3 Hackathon (35,502 participants). It opened on "Friday at 4:55 PM, the Red Sea crisis erupts" with "$50 million in cargo" at stake, and read satellite imagery alongside 500-page insurance policies in a 2M-token window ([Devpost](https://gemini3.devpost.com/); [Globot](https://devpost.com/software/globot-341w9q)).
- **SalesShortcut**, the exception, made up for having no unusual modality with 34 agents that placed real calls and sent real emails ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/salesshortcut)).

A third-party analysis of Kaggle's 50 Gemini 3 Pro winners found that nearly every one used video, voice or image input, and that **40 of the 50 were solo** ([analysis](https://adrianwwwang.github.io/Kaggle_Google_deepmind_winner_analysis/gemini3_winners_analysis.html)). Meta's own judges at LlamaCon gave Best Llama API Usage to Geo-ML, which used long context plus vision to turn 400-page geology reports into 3D subsurface models. They placed a frame-by-frame CCTV event detector third ([Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/llamacon-hackathon/)).

Compliance and fraud entries reach the podium at every event but rarely the top of it:

- At AWS, AegisAgent (insurance claims) took 2nd and Province (tax) took 3rd ([Devpost](https://aws-agent-hackathon.devpost.com/updates/38140-congratulations-to-the-winners-of-the-aws-ai-agent-global-hackathon)).
- Compliance Wizards placed 2nd at LlamaCon ([Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/llamacon-hackathon/)), and Compllama placed 2nd at Llama Impact SF ([lablab](https://lablab.ai/blog/llama-impact-hackathon-sf-summary)).
- At the IBM watsonx event, **four of the seven projects a judge showcased were compliance or risk gatekeepers**, the participant's own DORA Gatekeeper among them ([lablab/Ken Huang](https://lablab.ai/ai-articles/inside-the-agentic-ai-hack-with-ibm)).

The two compliance-flavoured projects that did take an overall first both framed compliance as a live risk event fed by non-text evidence: Globot, and RiskWise, which scanned shipping schedules and trade news to flag port delays and geopolitical risks for Microsoft ([Microsoft winners](https://microsoft.github.io/AI_Agents_Hackathon/es/winners/)). First places at the Anthropic × Cerebral Valley events went to domain experts who turned messy professional input into a deliverable. At Opus 4.6 that was CrossBeam, a personal-injury lawyer's housing-permit agent, chosen from 13,000+ applicants and 227 projects ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon); [CV newsletter](https://cerebralvalley.beehiiv.com/p/cerebral-valley-week-of-february-23rd)). At Opus 4.7 it included Wrench Board, which **draws its diagnosis directly onto the circuit board** ([EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/a-doctor-a-carpenter-and-a-teacher-win-anthropics-global-opus-47-hackathon)).

The story machine transfers from the Gatekeepers almost intact, and it is why that entry finished 2nd of roughly 8,000. Its parts are:

- the buyer's question as the title;
- a forcing function with a date and a penalty;
- pain expressed as volume × error × consequence;
- an agent with authority over a system of record;
- a visible red/green gate;
- remediation;
- a repo that looks like a product.

The IBM judge's stated lessons describe that recipe: focused high-value problems, real authority, quantified metrics, and "testing that verifies predictable, reliable behaviour" ([lablab/Ken Huang](https://lablab.ai/ai-articles/inside-the-agentic-ai-hack-with-ibm)).

What almost no winner supplied is the evidence machine. **None of the agent grand prizes reported precision, recall or any accuracy figure.** Province's "100% accuracy" meant 21 of 21 fields filled on one form ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/province)). The one plainly stated before/after metric in the sample, Gem-Care's word error rate of 19.0% against 32.7% for the base model, was explicitly "celebrated" by Google's judges ([blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/winning-entries-gemma-4-good-challenge/)). OpenAI's judges called the $50k gpt-oss red-teaming winner "exceptionally rigorous" ([EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/ucl-student-ilham-wicaksono-wins-50000-reward-at-global-openai-hackathon-with-holistic-ai)), and an outside critic faulted the Opus 4.6 winners for neglecting auditability ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260425-anthropic-hackathon)). Meta's judges are engineers who run a session called "Agents 102: Best Practices for Verifiable Agents". For them, measured evidence is a rare differentiator layered on top of wow, not a substitute for it (inference).

Four parts of the old recipe contradict the evidence and need replacing, not tuning:

1. **The lane.** A second text-centric finance-compliance checker would be crowded at a 20,000-participant event, and the genre has historically placed but not won (inference from the podium record above).
2. **Autonomy.** "Enforcement authority to block vendors without human approval" gives way to named, calibrated gates. Winners present these as features: Aegis's Protocol Zero asks for "Voice of God" approval on high-stakes actions, and Globot's Human On The Loop screen gates every critical decision ([Aegis](https://devpost.com/software/aegis-autonomous-multi-agent-crisis-command); [Globot](https://devpost.com/software/globot-341w9q)).
3. **Presentation.** The IBM deck-plus-TAM format gives way to a product video. Google's ADK and Kaggle rules show that only the opening minutes are reliably reviewed ([ADK](https://googlecloudmultiagents.devpost.com/)).
4. **Impact maths.** A headline like "$245M+ liability avoided" gives way to per-case numbers measured from traces. Engineers discount unexplained totals, and an engineer who reads the repo will notice the gap.

## Front doors are funded; the case after the alert is not

**AI-enabled cyber.** The money and the vendors cluster at the front door. FBI IC3 counted $20.877B in 2025 losses, and the $893M explicitly attributed to AI is described as "a floor, not a ceiling" ([SecureWorld](https://www.secureworld.io/industry-news/ai-enabled-fraud-topped-893m-fbi)). IBM's 2026 breach study found deepfake impersonation was the most common AI-powered attack type, and that AI involvement added roughly $1M per breach ([eSecurity Planet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/cybersecurity/ibm-2026-cost-of-a-data-breach-report-key-findings/)). These areas are saturated:

- **AI SOC alert triage.** 7AI raised a $130M Series A, and Gartner places AI SOC agents at the Peak of Inflated Expectations, warning that only 15% will see measurable gains without structured evaluation ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/agentic-security-firm-7ai-raises-130-million/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/09/prophet-security-evaluating-ai-soc-agents/)).
- **Email phishing.** It fell to **6%** of initial vectors "as automated controls improved" ([M-Trends 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026)).
- **Screenshot phishing classification.** Off-the-shelf multimodal models already identify the impersonated brand about 94% of the time ([arXiv 2511.09606](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09606)).
- **Brand takedown.** Netcraft claims a 33-minute median takedown, and Doppel raised at a valuation above $600M ([Netcraft](https://www.netcraft.com/blog/phishing-takedown); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/19/exclusive-doppel-raises-70-million-series-c-at-more-than-600-million-valuation-to-fight-ai-powered-social-engineering-attacks)).
- **Onboarding identity verification (IDV).** Entrust alone runs more than 1B verifications and reports that 1 in 5 biometric fraud attempts is a deepfake ([Entrust](https://www.entrust.com/company/newsroom/deepfakes-social-engineering-and-injection-attacks-on-the-rise)).
- **Real-time deepfake detection.** It is funded but brittle: open-source detectors lose about 50% of their AUC on in-the-wild video ([Deepfake-Eval-2024](https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.02857)).

What stays manual is the back office of each attack class. Nobody among GetReal, Nametag, Pindrop, Mirage or the ITDR vendors claims post-call help-desk forensics with containment ([GetReal](https://www.getrealsecurity.com/solutions/help-desk-social-engineering); [Mirage](https://www.miragesecurity.ai/blog/shinyhunters-vishing-playbook)). Nobody assembles deepfake-payment recall packs. Prompt injection carried in images or on screen is not covered by CSA's 2026 in-the-wild research ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-indirect-prompt-injection-in-the-wild-2026/)). Detecting deepfake job candidates is excluded outright, because it sits next to the "AI job-application screener" anti-project.

**Cyber GRC and regulatory reporting.** Volume and clocks are high and rising:

- 3,383 DORA major incidents in 2025 ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/2026/07/102nbyc/lessons-from-the-european-supervisory-authorities-first-dora-major-incident-repo));
- a September 2026 supervisors' list of recurring filing errors ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/DORA_Incident_reporting_-_Operational_instructions.pdf));
- CRA reporting, live since 11 Sep 2026 through a web-only portal ([Help Net Security](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/14/enisa-cra-single-reporting-platform)).

Three tooling layers are saturated:

- **Questionnaire and trust-centre tools.** Drata agreed to buy SafeBase for about $250M ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/drata-to-acquire-safebase-in-250-million-deal/)).
- **SOC 2 summarisers.**
- **AI-governance inventories.** The category now has its first Gartner Magic Quadrant, and the EU AI Act's high-risk duties have slipped to December 2027 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)).

Two more are partly solved. Basic evidence QA has shipped: Secureframe since May 2025, and Thoropass in preview ([Secureframe](https://secureframe.com/blog/announcing-ai-evidence-validation); [Thoropass](https://www.thoropass.com/company/newsroom/introducing-first-pass-ai-driven-evidence-verification)). Incident-reporting workflow is sold by RadarFirst and by DORA modules ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/01/radarfirst-ai-incident-management/); [Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/dora-compliance-software-buyers-guide.html)).

Three jobs remain open:

- extracting awareness time and impact facts from war-room audio and dashboards;
- grounded verification of walkthrough video;
- authenticity checks after the Delve allegations, under which 493 of 494 SOC 2 reports were nearly identical. Delve denies the allegations ([IANS](https://www.ians.com/news/delve-allegations-expose-weak-points-in-modern-compliance)).

**Financial crime and conduct.** Most of this sector is crowded:

- **AML alert triage** is the most crowded agentic category. Bretton AI raised a $75M Series B after more than 1.2M investigations ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260209387593/en/Bretton-AI-Raises-$75M-Series-B-Rebrands-from-Greenlite-AI-to-Build-the-AI-Standard-for-Financial-Crime)).
- **Communications surveillance** belongs to incumbents that own the capture pipes, and still produces about 100 false positives per real flag ([Mondo Visione](https://m.mondovisione.com/news/report-almost-one-third-of-firms-leveraging-ai-for-communications-surveillance-2025325)).
- **Chargebacks** are a $33.8B problem with a full vendor bench ([ChargebackStop](https://www.chargebackstop.com/blog/7-takeaways-from-ethocas-2025-state-of-chargebacks-report)).
- **Creator and finfluencer video compliance** is no longer underserved: Finspector, Sedric, PerformLine, Saifr and Theta Lake all claim video review ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/finspector-revamps-ai-to-police-finfluencer-promos); [Sedric](https://www.sedric.ai/blog/influencer-compliance); [PerformLine](https://performline.com/blog-post/video-marketing-compliance-financial-brands)). It is also the employer's own market. impact.com's SoFi case study reports a 24% drop in violations after replacing manual monitoring of partner URLs ([impact.com](https://impact.com/case-studies/sofi-automates-affiliate-compliance-affa-csb-imp-ev-nam/)).

The clear gap is **APP reimbursement adjudication**. Pay.UK's industry claims system handles workflow and calculators but neither makes nor recommends decisions ([Pay.UK RCMS outline](https://www.wearepay.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Pay.UK-APPR-RCMS-Product-Outline.pdf)).

**Trade, sanctions and supply chain.** Geopolitics creates real cliffs:

- the BIS Affiliates Rule returns on 10 Nov 2026; an extension to January 2027 was announced but not yet published as of early October ([Timewell](https://timewell.jp/en/columns/us-china-november-10-2026-convergence));
- IEEPA tariffs were struck down ([Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/update-on-ieepa-tariff-refunds));
- Section 301 forced-labour duty tiers now apply to 60 economies ([Crane](https://www.craneww.com/knowledge-center/trade-advisory-notices/new-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-take-effect-as-section-122-duties-expire/));
- CBP's EAPA programme found a record >$1B in evaded duties ([HSToday](https://www.hstoday.us/subject-matter-areas/customs-immigration/cbp-uncovers-record-1-billion-in-duty-evasion-under-enforce-and-protect-act/)).

But the screening core is commoditised, and the large penalties come from ownership and diversion failures, not from missed re-screens. Examples are GVA Capital at $216M and Applied Materials at $252.5M ([OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/civil-penalties-and-enforcement-information/2025-enforcement-information); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/03/bis-announces-252-5-million-settlement-with-applied-materials-over-alleged)). Maritime intelligence belongs to Windward and its peers ([Windward](https://windward.ai/news/windward-launches-remote-sensing-intelligence/)) and is the most conflict-adjacent theme in the whole landscape. HTS classification is crowded, and frontier LLMs score only 25–40% at the 10-digit level ([GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/hts-classification-accuracy)). The open job is auditing an importer's own UFLPA or origin evidence pack. CBP's 9 Jun 2026 guidance rejects untranslated documents and incomplete sub-tier information, and Tradeverifyd maps and organises evidence without auditing its content ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/new-compliance-tools-cbp-issues-comprehensive-forced-labor-guidance); [Tradeverifyd](https://tradeverifyd.com/resources/uflpa-compliance)).

## Compliance Shockwave fails the tooling test it set for itself

The PRD is unusually good verifiable-agent engineering, and that deserves saying before the critique. Its evidence contract reads `{source_type, source_id, page_or_record, excerpt_or_region, source_hash}`, and anything outside it is visibly marked `unverified`. The backend, not the model, verifies that official citations exist in source snapshots. AI output can never produce `hold_approved`. It seeds hard negatives (lookalike entities), keeps `needs_manual_review` distinct from `likely_match`, and refuses to collapse "unknown" into "negative". Its KPI table is more honest than any winner reviewed:

- affected-order recall rather than "legal violation recall";
- released capacity rather than cash savings;
- a "human approval integrity" metric whose expected value is exactly zero;
- separate live-call and fixture-replay indicators.

These are exactly the habits that Meta's "Agents 102: Verifiable Agents" session and constitution Article IX reward.

Under heavy scrutiny, the problem it applies them to does not hold up.

1. **The core loop is a commodity feature.** "List change → affected open orders → human-released hold" is what SAP GTS's C1/C2 delta screening does after each list load ([SAPinsider](https://sapinsider.org/improve-operational-effectiveness-by-properly-loading-and-monitoring-spl-data)). It is also what Descartes Dynamic Screening does daily ([Descartes](https://www.visualcompliance.com/compliance-solutions/denied-party-screening/dynamic-screening/)), and what e2open + Kharon do with automatic Partner Export Holds on affiliates owned 50% or more ([e2open](https://www.e2open.com/blog/operationalizing-bis-affiliates-rule-with-kharon-e2open)). The PRD's other claim, agentic investigation with an audit trail, shipped on 10 Sep 2026 as Castellum.AI's Arbiter inside LexisNexis Bridger. Castellum claims 95% of alerts resolved and 83% less review time ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/10/castellum-ai-brings-ai-inside-aml-screening/)).
2. **The losses sit elsewhere.** The big 2025–26 penalties came from failures the PRD defers to a "Should" or "stretch":
   - ownership look-through: IPI Partners accepted a $50M commitment through a trust-owned BVI vehicle, and Gracetown kept taking payments for two years after being told of a designation ([Barnes Richardson](https://www.barnesrichardson.com/ofac-imposes-large-russia-penalties-as-2025-comes-to-a-close));
   - diversion through subsidiaries: Applied Materials paid $252.5M ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/03/bis-announces-252-5-million-settlement-with-applied-materials-over-alleged)).
   
   "We didn't re-screen after the list changed" corresponds to the smallest penalties ([OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/civil-penalties-and-enforcement-information/2025-enforcement-information)).
3. **Meta tooling is not load-bearing.** Mixed-layout invoice extraction is generic document AI. SAM 3.1, video and perception grounding go unused, and Voice is a "Could". That is rung 3 of the constitution's ladder at best, and swapping in any other vision model would not break the product. This is the hard fail.
4. **The impact is small and hypothetical.** The PRD's ROI scenario is 12,000 packets × 11 minutes × $90/hour = **$198,000 a year**, far below the constitution's ≥$1B industry bar. It does not use the anchors that exist: "up to 99%" false positives and an estimated $15–25 per alert review, from a vendor-authored model ([Retail Banker International](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/comment/why-99-percent-sanctions-screening-alerts-false-positives/)).
5. **There is no perception moment.** The demo has no visible perceptual work: tables of synthetic orders, a dismissed lookalike and an approve button. That is the constitution's own red flag, "internally impressive, externally dull".
6. **Calibrated autonomy is missing.** Every case routes to a human, so there is no autonomy rate to report for Best Agentic (R2).
7. **The framing is risky.** OFAC programmes centre on conflict states, so the demo must scrub names and geographies to stay clear of "anything political or involving war".
8. **The evaluation is underpowered and the user is unfamiliar.** Five seeded cases cannot support P/R/F1, and the persona, a trade compliance analyst, is not a job the participant knows firsthand.

The pivots do not rescue the trade lane. **Pivot A**, the UFLPA/origin admissibility auditor, is the best version, and its multimodal ceiling is real. ## Three finalists under heavy scrutiny

### 1. Callback — "Where social engineering gets rolled back"

#### The user, the job and what the status quo costs

**User and job.** The user is the IAM or service-desk security lead at a large enterprise, or the managed security provider running an outsourced desk. Every morning they need to answer one question: which of yesterday's password, MFA and device-enrolment resets handed an account to an attacker, what did the attacker reach, and can we undo it before the disclosure clock starts?

**Why the status quo fails.** Today's verification is knowledge-based questions (employee ID, the manager's name, the agent "listening to the voice"), and AI-gathered OSINT and voice cloning defeat it ([Nametag](https://getnametag.com/newsroom/helpdesk-deepfake-security)). Nobody correlates the call with what happened next. In the 2025 UNC6040 Salesforce campaign, attackers posed as IT support by phone and had victims authorise a malicious connected app. Many victims learned of the breach only when the extortion email arrived ([Varonis](https://www.varonis.com/blog/salesforce-vishing-threat-unc604?hsLang=fr)).

**Cost of the status quo.**

- The UK's Cyber Monitoring Centre put the M&S/Co-op event at £270–440M and believes access came through "abuse of IT help desk processes" ([ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/security/cyber-attacks/cyber-attacks-on-uk-retailers-financial-impact)).
- MGM lost more than $100M to help-desk vishing in 2023 ([Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/scattered-spider-threat-actor-profile/)).
- Vishing caused **11%** of the intrusions Mandiant investigated in 2025, second only to exploits. Access hand-off between criminal groups fell to **22 seconds** ([M-Trends 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026)).
- Vishing- and smishing-initiated breaches had the **highest average cost of any initial vector at $5.29M** ([eSecurity Planet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/cybersecurity/ibm-2026-cost-of-a-data-breach-report-key-findings/)).

**Per-case evidence is thin, and every figure needs a label:**

- about $70 of labour per reset (Forrester, undated, as cited by a vendor);
- about 9,600 resets a year at a 10,000-employee firm (vendor model);
- about $8,500 to investigate each fraudulent reset (vendor model) ([Trusona](https://www.trusona.com/blog/the-70-call-a-help-desk-story));
- about $22 per Level-1 ticket, attributed to MetricNet via a secondary article ([SecurityMEA](https://securitymea.com/2025/09/11/how-much-does-a-password-reset-really-cost-a-company/)).

**No public source gives the minutes a post-call investigation takes; that number must be measured.**

**Forcing function and scaling.** The forcing function is the incident clock that starts at awareness: SEC Item 1.05 four business days after the materiality determination, a NIS2 early warning within 24 hours, and a DORA initial notice within 4 hours of classifying an incident as major ([CyberDefenders](https://cyberdefenders.org/blog/cyber-incident-reporting-deadlines/)). It scales across clients and industries naturally. Any enterprise with a service desk is a client, the retail, hospitality and SaaS-CRM victims above among them. Managed providers multiply reach, and ITSM and IdP marketplaces are the distribution channel. The roadmap extends the same fusion engine to contact-centre account takeover and to deepfake payment-approval calls (Recall Desk).

**Whitespace.** Every vendor checked works a neighbouring job:

- GetReal verifies callers in real time, and its response actions stop at "Calls can be terminated, escalated to specialists, or routed to fraud teams" ([GetReal](https://www.getrealsecurity.com/solutions/help-desk-social-engineering)).
- Nametag gives a pass/fail verdict inside ITSM workflows and deepened its ServiceNow partnership in June 2026 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260623298447/en/Nametag-Deepens-Partnership-with-ServiceNow-to-Protect-Enterprise-Helpdesks-Against-Impersonation-Attacks)).
- Pindrop scores contact-centre and meeting calls in real time ([IDTechWire](https://idtechwire.com/pindrop-embeds-deepfake-detection-and-voice-authentication-in-zoom-contact-center/)).
- Mirage runs simulated vishing calls for training ([Mirage](https://www.miragesecurity.ai/blog/shinyhunters-vishing-playbook)).
- ITDR tools and 7AI correlate identity alerts but do not list call recordings as an input ([Silverfort](https://www.silverfort.com/platform/identity-threat-detection-and-response/); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/agentic-security-firm-7ai-raises-130-million/)).

The residual gap sits between "before and during the call" and "after the identity alert". No one turns the recording and the agent's screen into forensic evidence, joins them to the IdP events that followed, and undoes the change. This verdict has two named blind spots: an unread SecurityWeek piece titled "Social Engineering Detection Moves Into the Live Conversation" ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/social-engineering-detection-moves-into-the-live-conversation/)), and unchecked 2025–26 release notes from Okta, Entra ID Protection, Push Security and SlashID.

**How the gate works.** The Two-Key gate auto-closes clearly benign resets, which should be the large majority (inference). It auto-executes only reversible containment, such as session revocation, and only when the score is above a threshold calibrated on held-out data *and* Second Look agrees. Factor rollback, account disablement and anything sent outside the company need a human. Meta's computer-use protocol will not proceed past a pending safety check until that check is explicitly acknowledged ([tooling reference §7](the organisers' tooling reference, kept outside this repository)). Callback routes that acknowledgement to the human approver, so the platform's own primitive becomes the second key.

**What Callback carries over from the PRD.** The PRD's state machine is adapted with the same actor-attribution rule: `case_opened → investigating → needs_review → (closed | contain_proposed) → (contained | rejected)`. Its evidence contract becomes `{source_type: call|screen|log, source_id, turnId|frame|event_id, excerpt_or_region, source_hash}`.

**Why Meta.** Each capability is load-bearing:

- Without diarization, the system cannot tell whether a phrase like "I'm travelling, new phone" came from the caller or the agent.
- Without grounding plus SAM, it cannot show that the agent skipped the verification panel and clicked "Reset MFA" on a specific user row. Logs record the reset but not the skipped check.
- Without 1M context, the week of logs needs a retrieval pipeline.
- Without computer use, there is no containment for consoles the SOC has no API scope for.

The **"only Meta" sentence** for form Q8: *"Callback has to hear who said what on the call, see what the agent clicked and follow that click to the pixel across the screen recording, hold a week of identity logs in one context, and undo the change through a console session that cannot proceed until a human acknowledges a safety check — Muse Voice Transcribe diarization, Muse Spark perception grounding, SAM 3.1 tracking and Spark computer use deliver all of that on one API key."*

**Caveats that shape the build.**

- Spark 1.3's audio input is "not fully supported", so the audio path is Voice Transcribe file mode: WAV mono 16-bit, at most 10 minutes and 32 MB, turn-level timestamps only.
- Meta's stack has no text-to-speech, so synthetic calls need the participant's own voices or a separately licensed TTS.
- No Meta model is a deepfake classifier. Callback must never claim to "detect deepfakes". A synthetic-voice score is at most a secondary external signal.

### 2. Claim Clock — "Same evidence, same decision, inside five days"

#### The user, the job and what the status quo costs

**User and job.** The user is the APP claims handler, and their head of claims, at a UK sending payment service provider. For each claim they must decide within five business days:

- whether it is in scope;
- whether the consumer-standard-of-caution exception or vulnerability applies;
- the amount;
- the split with the receiving firm.

Then they write the decision letter and populate the industry claims system.

**Volume.** The PSR's dashboard shows about **438,300 reported and 301,500 in-scope claims** between 7 Oct 2024 and 31 Mar 2026. Q1 2026 alone had 86,500 in-scope claims. £316m was reimbursed (88% of value), 82% of claims closed within five business days, and only 3% were rejected for insufficient caution ([PSR dashboard](https://www.psr.org.uk/information-for-consumers/app-scams-reimbursement-dashboard/)).

**The measurable failure is consistency.** Frontier Economics' evaluation for the PSR found that reimbursement by value **varied from 21% to 94% across PSPs**. One firm applied the caution exception to more than 25% of claim value while others never used it. PSPs bear £44–56m a year in costs beyond reimbursement ([Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/app-fraud-reimbursement-evaluation/)). APP losses reached £576.4m in 2025, up 19% ([FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/financial-crime-fraud-prevention/uk-payment-fraud-losses-topped-1-28bn-in-2025-new-uk-finance-report-finds)). In older data, the Ombudsman overturned more than three-quarters of bank APP decisions ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/252509369/Finance-ombudsman-overturns-more-than-three-quarters-of-bank-decisions-on-APP-fraud)). **No source gives bank-side minutes or cost per claim.**

**Forcing function and scaling.** A PSR consultation in December 2026 will cover claims unresolved after 35 days and caution guidance, with a decision expected in May 2027, while PSR functions move to the FCA ([Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/app-fraud-reimbursement-evaluation/)). The EU's PSR provisional agreement makes platforms liable to PSPs for scam content they were told about and did not remove ([William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/psr-psd3-eu-payment-services-legislation-is-agreed/)). Scaling runs across every UK sending and receiving PSP, then to EU PSPs. A second product line is platform-liability notices built from the same scam-ad evidence.

**Whitespace, the best verified of any finalist.** Pay.UK's RCMS provides workflow, inter-PSP messaging and reimbursement calculators, but its product outline describes no evidence upload, no caution fields and no decision recommendation ([Pay.UK](https://www.wearepay.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Pay.UK-APPR-RCMS-Product-Outline.pdf); [Payments Association](https://thepaymentsassociation.org/article/app-fraud-and-the-launch-of-the-rcms-platform-what-psps-need-to-do/)). The fraud vendors that surfaced, Form3 with Feedzai, and Salv, sell prevention ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/paytech/form3-and-feedzai-launch-industry-first-app-fraud-solution-for-the-uk-market)). A targeted search for adjudication products returned none. Feedzai, BioCatch and Hawk case-management modules were not checked individually.

### 3. First Notice — "One incident, every regulator, on the clock"

#### The user, the job and what the status quo costs

**User and job.** The user is the regulatory incident-reporting lead or major-incident manager at an EU or UK financial entity, or a product-security lead at a manufacturer under the CRA. Within hours, they must:

- fix the awareness time that starts every clock;
- classify the incident against each applicable regime's thresholds;
- draft the initial notifications;
- keep later versions consistent.

**Volume and failure.** The ESAs counted **3,383 DORA major incidents in 2025** (282 a month), a third of them cross-border ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/2026/07/102nbyc/lessons-from-the-european-supervisory-authorities-first-dora-major-incident-repo)). With an initial, at least one intermediate and a final report each, that implies **at least 10,000 DORA submissions a year** (inference). AFME says the larger cost is uncounted: triage of every *candidate* incident, run by 24/7 teams ([AFME](https://www.afme.eu/our-work/digital-innovation/ensuring-appropriate-and-workable-cyber-and-operational-resilience-requirement/dora-incident-reporting/)). Supervisors' September 2026 instructions list the errors entities keep making ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/DORA_Incident_reporting_-_Operational_instructions.pdf)):

- missing final reports;
- incident identifiers changed between versions, causing double counting;
- "critical services affected" not repeated;
- the home country listed in geographic spread;
- monetary fields not in thousands.

That list is a ready-made specification for the verifier. In the US, banks face at least ten federal cyber-reporting requirements ([BPI joint letter](https://bpi.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Joint-Financial-Trades-Reg-S-K-Cyber-Comment-Letter-4.10.26.pdf)), and some entities spend up to 50% of staff time on compliance ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-06-26_R49009_44d38bd3fdaf49f352dbfacf405ac383208504f4.pdf)). **Per-incident cost is unpublished.**

**Forcing functions.** These are the strongest of any finalist:

- the CRA has applied since 11 Sep 2026, with 24-hour early warnings, a web-only, English-only portal, and the warning that choosing the wrong coordinating CSIRT "can invalidate the notification" ([Help Net Security](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/14/enisa-cra-single-reporting-platform));
- NIS2 runs on 24-hour and 72-hour clocks, and four member states were referred to the CJEU in July 2026 over incomplete transposition ([Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/nis2-enforcement-tracker-2026.html));
- the UK Cyber Security and Resilience Bill sets 24h/72h clocks and is expected to become law by the end of 2026 ([Stevens & Bolton](https://www.stevens-bolton.com/insights/102mgn2/uk-cyber-security-and-resilience-bill-under-scrutiny/));
- CNIL fined Free's two companies a combined €42M, and its findings included individual notifications that lacked required information ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/en/sanction-free-2026)).

**This is the "geopolitical disruption of compliance" lens in its most buildable form.** The EU keeps adding regimes. The US is retreating: banks petitioned to rescind SEC Item 1.05 ([BPI](https://bpi.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Joint-Financial-Trades-Reg-S-K-Cyber-Comment-Letter-4.10.26.pdf)), and the CIRCIA final rule missed its own deadlines ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/cisa-plans-to-finalize-cyber-incident-reporting-regulations-in-september-2026)). The product must therefore treat each regime as a plug-in.

**Scaling and whitespace.** Clients span financial entities, NIS2 sectors, CRA manufacturers and GDPR controllers. The whitespace is *partial and unverified*. RadarFirst, OneTrust and the DORA modules run rules engines and workflow over facts that humans type in. None of the material reviewed extracts facts from bridge-call audio and dashboards.

## Conclusion

The participant's real edge is not compliance knowledge as such but the ability to make an agent's judgement auditable. The research shows the market's gap sits where auditability is hardest: **the case after the alert**, when the evidence is a phone call, a screen recording and a log trail rather than a document. Shockwave's best ideas belong there: a citation the backend, not the model, verifies; an approval state the model cannot set; KPIs that refuse to call capacity "savings". As the original recipe predicted, the domain was the variable and the pattern was the constant. What has changed is that the pattern now needs eyes and ears to compete for a grand prize.

Two further implications follow:

- **Geopolitics is the wrong lens for an eight-day solo build.** It reaches such a build less through sanctions screening, which is commoditised and conflict-adjacent, than through regulatory divergence and AI-industrialised fraud. The one open geopolitical job, auditing importers' forced-labour evidence packs, is real but blocked on data rights.
- **Evidence is the cheapest differentiator.** Almost none of the field's winners measured anything. A single honest PR curve and an autonomy rate at the two-minute mark are likely worth more to Meta's engineer-judges than another agent on the roster.
